Identity

An ERC-20 has no picture. What it can have is a metadataURI() that indexers read — the same getter this chain's other launchpads already expose, so filling this in is what makes your token show up with a face instead of a blank circle.

    What actually happens

    One call to createAndInitializePoolIfNecessary. It deploys the pool contract if that exact pair and fee has none, and writes the opening price. If the pool already exists it does nothing and costs almost nothing — you cannot overwrite someone else's price.

    The order of the pair is not yours to choose

    Uniswap sorts the two addresses and calls the smaller one token0. Arc's USDC starts with 0x36, so about one token in five sorts below it and the pair flips. The page computes it and tells you which way round yours landed.

    Never the native path

    The position manager here reports a WETH9() that does not answer name() — a dead address inherited from the fork it came from. Everything is done with plain ERC-20 approvals instead.

    Add liquidity

    Where you put the range matters more than how much you put in. Pick one and it will tell you what you just built.

    Shape

    Two approvals, then a mint

    The position manager moves both tokens, so each needs an allowance. This asks for exactly the amount you typed, never unlimited.

    You may not spend what you typed

    At a given price and range the pool decides the ratio it can accept. Whatever it cannot use stays in your wallet. The amounts are a ceiling, not a promise — which is also why a one-sided range only needs one token: the other side has nothing to do.

    Your tokens

    Tokens you deployed here, plus anything else you want to look at. Open one and it reads the deployed bytecode to find which functions really exist — not what the source claimed, what is actually on chain.

    How the scan works

    Every function has a 4-byte selector, and a contract that implements one has those bytes in its code. The page hashes a list of known signatures and searches the deployed bytecode for each. A match is strong evidence the function is there; an absence is near-proof it is not.

    It reads state, not promises

    Where a power exists, its current setting is read straight from the contract — who the owner is, whether it is paused, whether trading was ever switched on. That is the difference between what a token says and what it does.

    The list is local

    Tokens you deploy are remembered in this browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere, and clearing site data clears the list — the tokens themselves are on chain and unaffected.

    Your liquidity

    Every position this wallet holds, with what it is worth doing to it. V4 walls launched with the basey factory come first.

    Connect a wallet to see your positions.

    Add more

    Puts more into the same range, on the same NFT. The range itself cannot be moved — a different range is a different position.

    Lock for good (V4 walls)

    Sends the wall into the LaunchLocker through the factory: two signatures, and it can never be undone. The liquidity stays in the pool for good, and you keep collecting its fees.

    Lock with a deadline

    Compiles a locker in your browser, deploys it from your wallet, and sends the position into it. You keep collecting fees the whole time. Only time can release it, and the contract has no admin.

    Burn permanently

    Sends the position to a dead address. The liquidity can never be withdrawn by anyone, ever, and neither can the fees it earns from then on.

    Withdraw

    Pulls the liquidity out and collects what is owed.

    Sell wall

    One wall holds everything that is not your treasury, so nothing is left over. It holds only your token and needs no USDC: the price rises through it and it sells for you, at prices published on chain before anyone buys.

    First buy

    The price is computed, never typed

    You choose the market cap and the supply; the opening price falls out of them. Pick a billion or a quadrillion and it still opens at the same market cap — all that changes is how many zeros the price has.

    What you will sign

    One transaction per row, in order. It stops at the first failure rather than carrying on.

    The one thing to watch

    The wall should ask for tokens and no USDC. If it asks for USDC its range landed on the wrong side of the price — stop instead of signing.

    Why the first buy cannot be sniped

    On V4 the basey factory deploys the token, opens the pool, places the wall and buys in one call. On V3 a one-shot contract, compiled here like the locker, creates the pool, places the wall and buys in its constructor. Either way it is one transaction: there is no moment in which the pool exists without your buy. Neither keeps anything or has an owner, and you approve the exact USDC of the buy.

    Locked for good (V4)

    The wall goes into the LaunchLocker, openlaunch.lol's contract copied as is. It has no function that takes liquidity out, so the wall stays in the pool for the life of the chain, and collect() pays you its trading fees. Trackers count that liquidity as burned.

    What you launched

    Your tokens and the positions behind them, with everything you can do to them from here.

    Collect fees

    Trading fees accrue separately from the money you put in, so collecting them never touches your liquidity — and it works even while a position is locked.

    Add or withdraw

    Adding puts more into the same range on the same NFT. Withdrawing pulls the liquidity out and collects what is owed. A locked position can do neither until its date.

    Withdrawing is not free

    The USDC sitting in a position is what a buyer sells back into. Taking it out is the same act as removing the exit, whatever the intention — selling treasury tokens raises money without closing that door.

    Your fast wallet

    Everything on this site is signed by a fast wallet derived from one signature of yours. That is why nothing here opens a wallet popup: the key is yours and it is in this page. Your main wallet signs exactly one thing — putting money in.

    The cluster

    Wallets derived from the fast one, so a single signature brings the whole set back on any machine. They are ordinary addresses: no sign-up, no fee, no limit. They sign locally, so moving between them opens nothing.

    Nothing here polls on its own — Arc's nodes are down for a good part of the day and a page that keeps asking is a page that keeps failing.

    Buy and sell with them

    Paste an address and pick who buys. Each wallet is quoted fresh, right before its own trade — they hit the same pool one after another, so the second one gets a worse price than the first and a quote taken up front would be a lie by the time it was used.

    Our launchpad

    One transaction creates the coin, opens its Uniswap pool and puts the liquidity in. You do not pick the range, the wall or the first buy: the contract does. It opens at $5,000, graduates at $50,000, charges 1% and splits the fees 80% to you and 20% to the platform.
